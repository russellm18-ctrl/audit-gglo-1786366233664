## Atmospheric Reality Gap

The four images attached tell a coherent, high-conviction story: warm Corten steel curves catching afternoon light, an aerial courtyard composition where geometry and planting create genuine visual tension, a timber-and-glass cultural center photographed with a single figure scaled to make the architecture feel monumental, and a splash-pad frame that converts a public amenity into lived human joy. This is photography that earns the headline "Connecting people through beauty and innovation" — the visual evidence matches the verbal promise.

The trust cost is not in the images themselves. It is in what a buyer encounters the moment they try to go deeper. The photography opens a door; the infrastructure slams it shut.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

13 of 22 homepage links are unreachable. That is not a maintenance footnote — that is a broken buyer journey at every decision point. `gglo.com/about/` is dead. `gglo.com/people/` is dead. `gglo.com/contact/` is dead. The three project links surfaced in the hero carousel — `1101-western`, `the-schoolhouse-district`, and `africatown-plaza` — all return unreachable. A developer relations director or a municipal procurement officer who clicks "Connected urban livability" or "Revitalizing community identity" from the hero gets a dead page. The photography primes intent; the broken link erases it and sends that buyer to a competitor whose site actually opens.

The homepage root itself — `https://www.gglo.com` — is flagged unreachable in the scan, meaning the domain's www-redirect may be intermittently failing. When the front door itself is broken, no amount of excellent photography recovers the impression.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

86% of the 7 content images on the homepage carry no alt text — 6 missing out of 7. That means the Africatown Plaza image (`2018123_01_N28-500x500.jpg`), the WSU Cultural Center image (`2015031_00_N54_webthumb-500x500.jpg`), and the others are invisible to search crawlers and screen readers alike. The filenames are timestamp-prefixed project codes with no descriptive language: `2017032_00_900_Albert_N8_cropped_800x800-500x500.jpg` tells Google nothing about mixed-use architecture in Seattle.

The page title is "Home - GGLO" — 11 characters. The meta description is missing entirely. The `og:image` tag is missing, which means every LinkedIn share, every Slack paste, every email with a link previews as a blank tile. For a firm whose entire value proposition is visual, sharing a link that renders no image is a self-inflicted credibility wound.

GGLO ranks #1 organically for its own brand name — that position is holding. But with no meta description, Google is auto-generating preview text from whatever body copy it finds first, likely the cookie consent block. That is the sentence currently auditioning GGLO to every prospect who searches the firm by name.

The photography is doing its job. The infrastructure is not. Every broken link and missing alt tag is a gap between what a buyer sees in the first three seconds and what they can act on in the next thirty.
